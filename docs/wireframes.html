Process flow

Seven steps from an uploaded CSV to an exported approved list. The spine is the happy path; boxes to the left are refusals that write nothing, boxes to the right are the records the flow reads and writes. Step 5 loops once per approval level.

BMD user action Platform / Apex Approver action Record store / read source Refused — nothing written
  • Status lives on the invitee, not the event. The event shows roll-up counts, so one AM's pending batch never blocks another's.
  • The chain is stamped whole, not walked. All five approver fields are resolved at submit and frozen, so a reorganisation mid-approval cannot reroute an item somebody is already looking at.
  • Every step of this is built at R9, including the chain. It routes on the org chart, not on a route table — see the next section for how a chain is resolved.

Approval chain

Who signs is resolved once, at submit, from the org chart: level 1 is the Account Owner behind the invitee, and each level above is that person's manager, up to the level cap or the first Regional Head — whichever comes first. Two invitees on the same event routinely have different chain lengths, which is why the approver's screen says Level 2 of 3 rather than assuming.

No chain, so the whole batch is refused — with a named error, nothing written, and the rows left in Draft. The remedy is data: an account kept for non-customer guests, whose owner becomes their level 1.

  • The cap and the title markers are one Custom Metadata record, Approval_Chain_Setting__mdt.Default — raising the cap to 3 or 4 starts populating Approver_4__c and Approver_5__c with no deploy.
  • Title matching is a case-insensitive substring, because real titles carry a region. That also matches “Assistant to the Regional Head”, which is why the marker is a setting an admin can narrow rather than a string in the code.
  • Unverified from this repo: whether this org populates User.Title at all. An unmatched title does not error — every chain simply climbs to the cap, so the symptom is approvals taking one signature longer than expected.
  • What it costs: the chain can no longer be edited as data. Changing who signs means changing the org chart, which is an administrator's Setup change rather than a row an operations team can add.

Upload a CSV (.csv) file — comma, semicolon or tab separated. Everyone in it becomes an Event Attendee, ready to be invited to any event. Expected headers: First Name, Last Name, Email, Company (Title and Mobile optional).

Nothing else in the org is touched: no contact, lead or account is created, changed or read. People already imported are recognised by name, company and email, so re-uploading a file updates them instead of duplicating them.

Save it as UTF-8 (in Excel: File → Save As → “CSV UTF-8”), otherwise accented names cannot be read.

Each invitee records who added it. You submit only your own batch — other AMs' selections are unaffected. Submitting sends each invitee to the account manager who owns their customer, and up the approval chain from there.

After Submit Toast

slds-notify_toast · the only place an AM meets the chain

Submitted. 14 invitee(s) sent for approval — 3 approver(s) notified at level 1. The longest chain is 3 levels — each level decides in turn, and you hear once every one of them has.
✕
  • Grouped by company text, because an attendee has no Account behind them. Two spellings of one organisation group separately, and the screen does not pretend otherwise.
  • Selections survive a filter change, so the component says out loud when the Add count includes rows that are off screen — a count silently disagreeing with the table is worse than a note.
  • Both buttons carry counts; Submit is the brand button because it is the one irreversible act on this tab.
  • The chain sentence appears only when a chain is longer than one level — on the common case it would be noise, and on the uncommon one it is the whole news.
  • A batch is all-or-nothing. One invitee that reaches no Account Owner refuses the whole submission, including the rows that could have been routed. Nothing moves out of Draft.
  • The helper text names the first approver, not the whole ladder. It used to read “sends each invitee to your manager”, which R9 made wrong; an AM needs to know who gets it first, and the toast supplies the depth once the chain is actually resolved.

The platform's own approval surface: the bell, the email, the Approvals list, the record lock and the audit trail. It works everywhere, but it cannot group by company — which is exactly what Screen 4b exists for.

  • The rows are work items, not invitees. Company, title and remark live on the target record, so this list cannot show or sort by them at all — the reason Screen 4b was built.
  • Approvers set Receive Approval Request Emails = Never; the one aggregated email from EventNotificationService is the notification instead of 40 per-item mails.
  • Levels 2 and up are told when their turn begins, not at submit. Each step's approval action advances Current_Level__c, and the Invitee_Level_Advanced flow turns that into one aggregated notice per approver per event — without it, a Regional Head would never hear anything at all.
  • A rejection at any level ends the whole request; no level above is asked.

  • Nothing starts selected. Pre-ticking every row would make "approve everybody" the path of least resistance, which inverts what an approval is for.
  • “Level 1 of 3” is deliberately quieter than VIP. VIP is a claim about the guest and should catch the eye; the level is context about the decision — whether this signature is the last one — and must not compete for attention on every row. It is hidden entirely on a one-level chain.
  • Two invitees on one event legitimately show different chain lengths, because a Regional Head ends a chain wherever they appear. Nordbank's owner is three rungs below one; Helvetia's reports to one directly.
  • “Waiting on me” is read from ProcessInstanceWorkitem, not from a field comparison. Approver_2__c is populated from the moment of submission, so a field test would let a level-2 approver act on work that level 1 has not released yet.
  • The Not a customer group can no longer hold pending rows. Routing starts at the Account Owner, so a guest with no Contact link is refused at submit and never reaches this screen. The branch survives in the template from R8; the guests it was drawn for now need an account kept for them, which is a manual step in DEPLOYMENT.md.
  • Remark and VIP sit on the row, because they are the AM's case for inviting this person, and the approver is the reader they were written for.
  • Capped at 200 invitees per decision: refusing at a stated number beats a governor limit surfacing as an unexplained error.
